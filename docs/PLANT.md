# Plant model

How the 100 MW Pavagada plant's output is **modelled** (there is no metered data for it), and how the model was calibrated. Numbers: `PLANT_CALIBRATION.md` (generated by `scripts/plant_model.py`). Code: `src/solarschedule/plant.py`.

## The model
```
AC (MW) = 100 × min( dc_ac × POA/1000 × (1 + γ × (T_mod − 25)), 1 )
T_mod   = T_air + POA / u
```
- **POA** (sunlight on the panels): pvlib sun position at the **midpoint** of each 15-min block (Open-Meteo labels the interval end), Hay-Davies transposition of Open-Meteo GHI/DNI/DHI onto a fixed rack tilted 14° facing south (about latitude tilt; Pavagada blocks are mostly fixed-tilt).
- **dc_ac, γ, u** come from the Kaggle plant. `dc_ac` is the fitted output at 1000 W/m² and 25 °C over the fitted clip level, so it is the effective DC/AC ratio **after** all real losses (wiring, mismatch, inverter, soiling during those 34 days).
- 100 MW is the AC limit and the "available capacity" in Reg 6(2).

## Calibration
Kaggle has, per 15 minutes, panel irradiance and module temperature from a sensor and AC power from each of 22 inverters. Per timestamp the model uses **mean AC per reporting inverter**, so an inverter dropout does not look like a cloud.

Fit on the first 24 days, tested on the last 10 (daylight rows):

| | Plant 1 | Plant 2 |
|---|---|---|
| DC/AC | 1.18 | 1.10 |
| γ (per °C) | −0.36% | −1.0% (at the bound) |
| MAE, % of capacity | **1.8** | 7.0 |
| Energy error | −1.0% | −2.6% |

**Plant 1 is used.** Its γ (−0.36%/°C) and DC/AC (1.18) are what a crystalline-silicon plant of that era should show, and the fit holds out of sample. Plant 2 is rejected: it reports low or zero output at full sun (10 timestamps above 1000 W/m², mean 631 kW per inverter, minimum 0), which is outages, not weather; the fit pushes γ to its bound to absorb them. Using modelled instead of measured module temperature adds only 0.04 points of MAE, which matters because Pavagada has no module sensor.

## What counts as "actual" at Pavagada
- **Base:** the Open-Meteo 15-minute historical forecast (`hist15`, short-lead forecasts stitched together) → model → `data/derived/actual_15.parquet`, column `mw`.
- **Check:** ERA5 reanalysis (hourly, spread to 15 min by the clear-sky shape) → column `mw_era5`. The two agree closely (correlation 0.98 on daylight blocks; mean absolute difference 4.6% of capacity).

Modelled capacity factor is 25–27% (AC). That is above the low-20s CUF usually reported for Indian fixed-tilt plants, most likely because Open-Meteo sunlight runs high under Indian aerosol haze, and because the model has no plant outages, grid curtailment or degradation. It is **not** tuned down: there is no Pavagada output to tune to. ₹ losses scale with output, so they may be slightly high; stated in the limitations.

## Hourly forecasts to 15-minute blocks
Day-ahead forecasts are hourly. `to_15min` spreads each hour over its four blocks using the clear-sky index: value ÷ clear-sky mean over the hour × clear-sky value of each block. This keeps the hour's energy exactly (tested) and makes sunrise and sunset ramp with the sun. Holding the hourly value flat instead would create fake deviation in the morning and evening blocks, the very blocks insight 3 is about.

## Limits
- One real plant, 34 days in May–June 2020, location unknown: it gives the *shape* of weather → power, not Pavagada's.
- No wind in the Kaggle data, so the temperature model ignores wind (a fixed u).
- No soiling cycle, degradation, outages or curtailment.
